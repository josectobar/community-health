SMART objectives

A strong objective is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. Skim the reference, then test the difference between objective types.

S
Specific

Exactly who and what.

M
Measurable

A number you can track.

A
Achievable

Realistic with your resources.

R
Relevant

Tied to the real goal.

T
Time-bound

A clear deadline.

Three flavors of objective

Process — measures the activities you carry out.
e.g., "Distribute 500 flyers at the packing sheds by August 30."
Impact — measures changes in knowledge, attitudes, or behavior.
e.g., "Increase the share of workers who drink water hourly from 40% to 70% in 6 months."
Outcome — measures long-term change in health status.
e.g., "Reduce heat-illness ER visits among workers by 20% within two years."

    How to nail the written responses

    The activity's Part 4 has multi-part prompts worth 3 points each. These habits earn full credit — open each tip.

    Answer every numbered part
    Each prompt has parts (1), (2), (3). Graders look for all of them. Number your answer the same way so nothing gets missed.
    Be specific to Rio Lindo
    Generic answers lose points. Name the packing sheds, the Spanish-speaking families, the bus stops, the heat — tie your answer to this community's real details.
    Write in complete sentences
    Bullet fragments are hard to grade and often incomplete. Full sentences show your reasoning.
    When asked to rewrite a message, keep it plain
    Short sentences, everyday words, one clear action. Picture a tired worker reading it in 5 seconds.
    When asked "how would you know it's working?"
    Name something you could actually observe or measure — attendance, a count, a before/after survey, a teach-back. Avoid vague answers like "people will like it."
