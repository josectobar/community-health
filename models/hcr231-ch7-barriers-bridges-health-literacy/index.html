Barriers, Bridges & Health Literacy

Two skills, two tabs. Sort obstacles into the right family, then appraise a document for how much health literacy it demands. Every scenario and excerpt here is different from the ones you will be assessed on.

The three categories

  • Communication barrierBlocks a patient from understanding or acting on health information — language, jargon, low health literacy, mistrust.
  • Access / structural barrierBlocks care itself — cost, transportation, distance, policy, fear tied to immigration status.
  • Communication bridgeA strategy or resource that closes the gap — plain language, visuals, interpreters, community health workers, peer educators, mobile clinics, culturally competent care.

The five-criterion checklist

  1. Plain language vs. medical jargon — clinical words a patient wouldn't use at home?
  2. Sentence length — short and direct, or long with stacked clauses?
  3. Reading level required — elementary, middle school, or college?
  4. Clear action step — does the reader know exactly what to do next?
  5. Design / visual support — headings, bullets, images, white space, a phone number?
