This framework was groundbreaking because it challenged the idea that health is determined primarily by medical care or individual behavior. It drew attention to the social and environmental forces that shape health long before a person ever enters a clinic.

Three levels of prevention describe different points at which health education specialists can intervene. Identifying which level your approach targets adds precision and professional depth to your reasoning.

Primary

Stopping a health problem before it occurs.

Examples: vaccination programs, health education campaigns, community fitness initiatives

Secondary

Early detection and intervention to reduce the impact of a problem that has already begun.

Examples: screenings, early diagnosis, risk-reduction programs

Tertiary

Managing and minimizing the effects of an existing condition to prevent further decline.

Examples: chronic disease management programs, rehabilitation, support groups

Quick test yourself

When you identify stakeholders, community assets, or program strategies in your discussion post, think about which level of prevention your approach targets — and say so. This shows professional precision in your reasoning.
