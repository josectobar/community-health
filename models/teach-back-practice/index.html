◇ The problem

The kidney waitlist

More than 90,000 people in the U.S. are waiting for a kidney transplant. Many wait years on dialysis — a demanding treatment that filters the blood several times a week. Demand far outstrips the supply of human donor kidneys.

90,000+ waiting · years-long waits
◇ The idea

What "xenotransplantation" means

It's transplanting an organ from another species into a human. Here, that's a kidney from a pig that has been genetically modified — its genes edited — to lower the chance the human body rejects it.

Cross-species · gene-edited pig organ
◇ The status

Where it stands today

It is still experimental. The first FDA-authorized human clinical trials began in 2025. A few patients have received pig kidneys under special access — some worked for weeks to a couple of months. It is not a proven, everyday option you can schedule. There are also real risks: the body can still reject the organ, and there's concern about diseases passing from animal to human across species.

Experimental · rejection & cross-species risk
◇ Your job

Why this is a communication challenge

Your task is to listen to the patient, answer their questions in plain language, and check that they understand. You don't need to be a medical expert — you help the patient feel part of the decision and make their own informed choice.

Listen · plain language · informed consent
