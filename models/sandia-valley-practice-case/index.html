The case (open this first, and reopen it any time)

Sandia Valley Health Alliance runs a transitional care program for adults hospitalized with heart failure. The program is six months old and covers one hospital, two city clinics, and one clinic in a town 50 miles away.

The team: a nurse care coordinator at each clinic, a clinical pharmacist, a home health liaison, a social worker, primary care providers, and a cardiology group that covers all sites.

What is in place: a shared electronic health record across the hospital and clinics, a discharge call within 48 hours, a home visit for patients judged high risk, and a monthly program meeting.

Six month results:

  • Thirty day readmissions moved from 22 percent to 20 percent.
  • Eighty one percent of patients received the discharge call on time.
  • Forty percent of eligible patients received the home visit.
  • Patients report receiving different weight and fluid instructions from the hospital and from the clinic.
  • Forty four percent of patients can name who to call when symptoms change.
  • Staff survey: cardiology is easy to reach and staff say they respect each other. Staff also say nobody is clearly in charge of the plan after discharge, the home health agency cannot see clinic notes, and the monthly meeting is too infrequent to catch problems.
  • The distant clinic lost its only pharmacy in April, and many of its patients have unstable housing.

The chief medical officer asks you, the care coordination quality lead, what these numbers mean.
