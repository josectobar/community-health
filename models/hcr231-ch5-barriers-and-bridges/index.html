Barriers and bridges in Copper Ridge

Spotting what blocks communication between a health professional and a community — and what opens it back up — is a judgment you have to make in every community you work in. Practice it here on Copper Ridge. Decide whether each detail gets in the way or builds a way in.

The case

Copper Ridge is a former mining town of about 3,800 residents in Gila County. Nearly a third of residents are over 65, broadband service is unreliable outside the town center, and the nearest hospital is 40 miles away. County data show diabetes-related hospitalizations climbing for the third year. You are a health education specialist newly assigned to launch a diabetes prevention program, and you do not know anyone there.

Sort each detail

Choose Barrier or Bridge for all ten, then check your answers. Nothing is submitted or graded — this is practice.

What to take away

  • A barrier is usually something the program is doing — the time, the place, the language, the words. It is rarely proof that the community does not care.
  • A bridge borrows trust that already exists in the community, or hands real decisions to residents.
  • A bridge only counts if it addresses the barrier you named. A clearer handout does not solve a problem of access.
