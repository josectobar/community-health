First F

Fluidity — How well understood is the problem?

Fluidity asks: How much do we already know about this community's health problem and its causes? It describes the state of your knowledge before planning begins.

Think of fluidity as a spectrum:

  • Low fluidity — The community is understudied. Root causes are unclear. You need a framework with strong exploratory assessment built in. MAPP's four-assessment approach is well-suited here because it gathers data from multiple angles simultaneously.
  • Moderate fluidity — Some data exists but community perspectives and system performance are unknown. Both frameworks can apply, depending on scope.
  • High fluidity — The health problem and its causes are well-documented. You are ready to design a targeted intervention. PRECEDE-PROCEED's structured phase-by-phase diagnosis works well when the problem is already defined.

The key question to ask yourself: Am I still trying to discover what the problem is, or do I already know and need to figure out how to address it?

Second F

Flexibility — What is the scope and complexity of the planning task?

Flexibility asks: Are we planning one specific program, or coordinating a broad community health improvement strategy across many organizations and priorities?

  • Narrow scope — One health problem, one target population, one intervention. PRECEDE-PROCEED is designed for this. It goes very deep on one specific problem — analyzing behaviors, environments, and the three PER factors in detail.
  • Broad scope — Multiple health priorities, multiple sectors, multiple organizations that all need to coordinate. MAPP is designed for this. It produces a community health improvement plan that can guide many organizations simultaneously.

The key question to ask yourself: Am I solving one specific problem, or building a system-wide plan that addresses the health of an entire community?

Important: Broad scope does not mean PRECEDE-PROCEED is irrelevant. In practice, MAPP is often used to identify community-wide priorities, and PRECEDE-PROCEED is then used to design the specific programs that address each priority. They are complementary.

Third F

Functionality — Who is driving the process, and what must the end product accomplish?

Functionality asks: What does the end product need to do, and who has the authority and capacity to implement it?

  • Program-level functionality — A health department or program team needs a plan for a specific intervention they will implement. PRECEDE-PROCEED produces exactly this: a detailed program plan with clear PER factor analysis and evaluation structure.
  • System-level functionality — A multi-sector coalition needs a shared agenda that coordinates many organizations across many years. MAPP produces a Community Health Improvement Plan (CHIP) — a living document that is formally adopted by the community and updated over time.
  • Community ownership — If the end product must be owned and driven by the community rather than by a single agency, MAPP's partnership-first approach is essential. A plan that is imposed rather than co-created rarely sustains itself.

The key question to ask yourself: Who will implement this plan, and what kind of document do they need to do that work effectively?

PRECEDE-PROCEED vs. MAPP: Side-by-Side

Use this table as a quick reference when deciding which framework fits your planning situation.

Feature PRECEDE-PROCEED MAPP
Primary purpose Diagnose a specific health problem deeply, then design and evaluate a targeted intervention Coordinate a community-wide health improvement strategy across multiple organizations and priorities
Scope One health problem, one intervention, one population Multiple health priorities, whole community, many sectors
Who leads it Health planners, program designers, research teams A broad multi-sector coalition including community residents
Starting point Community quality-of-life concerns (Phase 1) Building the partnership (Phase 1)
Diagnostic depth Very deep — analyzes predisposing, enabling, and reinforcing factors Broad — uses four different assessment lenses
Community strengths Focused primarily on needs and risk factors Explicitly identifies community assets and strengths (CTSA)
End product A detailed program plan with evaluation built in A Community Health Improvement Plan (CHIP) adopted by the coalition
Evaluation structure Three distinct levels: process, impact, and outcome Built into the Action Cycle (plan, do, check, act)
Best used when You know the problem and need a structured, evidence-based program to address it You need a multi-year, multi-sector community health improvement plan
Used together? Yes — MAPP identifies priorities; PRECEDE-PROCEED designs the programs that address them. They are complementary tools, not competing alternatives.
