Same program, different result

A care coordination program lowers readmissions downtown and shows no change at the rural clinic. Write your own answer first, then open the response and compare. Your writing stays in your browser and is not submitted.

1. Name three context questions you would ask before deciding the program failed at the rural site.

Compare with a strong response
Strong questions look at transportation and distance, at broadband and whether documentation happens in real time, at staffing and caseload, at patient complexity and how many patients live alone, and at whether the two sites enrolled the same kind of patient to begin with. Any one of these can mute an effect the program really did produce.

2. The rural site enrolls sicker patients who live farther from care. What does the Organizational Design Model predict?

Compare with a strong response
Coordination needs are higher there, so the same coordinating capacity produces a poorer fit and weaker results without anyone performing badly. The response is to change the mechanisms or add capacity, not to cancel the program.

3. The board wants one number to decide funding. What do you tell them?

Compare with a strong response
One number cannot separate a weak program from a strong program working in a hard context. A defensible answer pairs an outcome measure with process measures, a measure reported by patients, and a plain description of the context, so the board can see what the number stands on.
