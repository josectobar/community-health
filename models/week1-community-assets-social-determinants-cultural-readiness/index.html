Community Assets, Social Determinants & Cultural Readiness

Social determinants of health (SDOH) are the conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work, and age. They often have a greater impact on health outcomes than individual behaviors or clinical care alone.

Economic stability

Income, employment, financial security

Education & literacy

Schooling level, health literacy, access to learning

Healthcare access

Service availability, transportation, insurance

Housing & neighborhood

Housing quality, neighborhood safety, food access

Social connections

Support networks, community connectedness

Real-world example

A community member may fully understand that managing their diabetes requires dietary changes — but if they live in a food desert, work two jobs, or cannot afford medication, that knowledge alone will not be enough.

Why this matters for program design

Understanding SDOH means you design programs that meet people where they actually are — not where you assume them to be.

Culture is not a barrier to work around — it is a context to work within. Four key cultural factors shape whether a health program will actually be used:

Language

Materials or programs delivered only in English may exclude significant portions of a community, reducing both access and trust.

Religious or spiritual beliefs

These may shape attitudes toward certain medical treatments, preventive care, or even the concept of illness itself.

Community norms

Expectations around gender roles, family decision-making, or help-seeking behavior can influence whether someone engages with a health program at all.

Historical mistrust of healthcare

Communities that have experienced discrimination or neglect by healthcare institutions may approach new programs with skepticism — and rightfully so.

A health education specialist who ignores these factors risks designing a program that is culturally irrelevant, unintentionally offensive, or simply unused.
