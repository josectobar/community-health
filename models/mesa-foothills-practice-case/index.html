The case (open this first, and reopen it any time)

Mesa Foothills Family Medicine is a four provider clinic in Maricopa County. Leadership has announced that the clinic will implement a coordinated care model for its most complex patients. Nothing has changed yet in how the clinic works.

The patient: Rosa Delgado is 79 and lives in Mesa with her husband Arturo, who is 81 and manages her care. She has moderate dementia, diabetes, and arthritis. After a urinary tract infection and a fall last month she spent four days in the hospital.

Since discharge:

  • She came home with seven medications, two of them new, and Arturo is not sure which of the old ones to stop.
  • She has five appointments in the next three weeks across three locations.
  • The couple gave up driving last year. Their daughter lives in Flagstaff and visits monthly.
  • Arturo has lost weight, sleeps four hours a night, and told the nurse he does not know how much longer he can do this.
  • A home health nurse visits twice a week and has no way to see the clinic notes.
  • Rosa says what she wants is to stay in her own house and to keep going to church on Sunday.

At the clinic: no one is assigned as a coordinator, discharge follow up happens when someone remembers, there is no shared plan of care, and referrals to community agencies are made by handing out phone numbers. The clinic manager asks you how to start.
