    Part 2 — Four ethical principles

    A community health worker learns that the family has been sharing a neighbor's leftover inhaler, and is asked to keep it private. Use these definitions to decide which principle sits on each side of that decision.

    Autonomy

    Respecting a person's right to make their own decisions about their life and their information.

    Beneficence

    Acting to do good and to benefit the person.

    Nonmaleficence

    Avoiding harm.

    Justice

    Treating people fairly and distributing resources equitably.

    Part 3 — Seven steps for managing implicit bias

    From Chapter 10. Read all seven, then apply two of them to a professional who wrote that a family "seems disengaged" after one meeting.

    Step 4 catches people off guard. "See differences" does not mean judge them. It means the opposite of saying you do not notice race or culture — because pretending differences are not there also erases the conditions that create disparities.
